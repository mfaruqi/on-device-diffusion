## Findings

- [findings/a100-sxm4-runs-faster-than-a100-pcie.md](findings/a100-sxm4-runs-faster-than-a100-pcie.md) `supported`: The same sd.cpp configuration runs 5.6% faster on Gilbreth's A100-SXM4-40GB nodes than on A100-PCIE-40GB nodes; the two must not share a device label.
- [findings/bf16-gemm-time-matches-across-pytorch-and-sdcpp.md](findings/bf16-gemm-time-matches-across-pytorch-and-sdcpp.md) `supported`: With identical BF16 weights, per-step GEMM time on A100-PCIE is within ~7% between PyTorch (cuBLAS) and sd.cpp (cuBLAS via ggml).
- [findings/ggml-flash-attention-4x-slower-than-sdpa-flash-at-4608-tokens.md](findings/ggml-flash-attention-4x-slower-than-sdpa-flash-at-4608-tokens.md) `supported`: For FLUX.2 klein's attention shape (B1, H24, S4608, D128) on A100-PCIE, ggml's F16 flash-attention kernel takes ~4× the time of PyTorch SDPA-flash.
- [findings/ggml-fp32-activations-add-conversion-and-idle-time.md](findings/ggml-fp32-activations-add-conversion-and-idle-time.md) `supported`: sd.cpp's FP32-activation design adds ~37 ms of dtype conversion, ~12 ms of host-device copies and ~20 ms of GPU idle per denoise step compared with PyTorch.
- [findings/pytorch-denoise-step-third-in-unfused-memory-bound-ops.md](findings/pytorch-denoise-step-third-in-unfused-memory-bound-ops.md) `supported`: In the PyTorch reference on A100-PCIE, about a third of each FLUX.2 klein denoise step is unfused memory-bound ops; GEMMs already run near peak and the GPU is almost never idle.
- [findings/sdcpp-starts-faster-than-pytorch.md](findings/sdcpp-starts-faster-than-pytorch.md) `supported`: sd.cpp loads FLUX.2 klein 2.6× faster than PyTorch and its first generation is only ~7% slower than a warm one, versus 2.55× for PyTorch.
- [findings/sdcpp-vae-decode-dominated-by-im2col.md](findings/sdcpp-vae-decode-dominated-by-im2col.md) `supported`: In sd.cpp, two thirds of FLUX.2 klein's VAE decode GPU time at 1024² is the im2col step of its convolutions; the decode is 3.65× slower than PyTorch's.
- [findings/text-encoder-half-of-weights-little-of-time.md](findings/text-encoder-half-of-weights-little-of-time.md) `supported`: In FLUX.2 klein the Qwen3 text encoder is half of the BF16 weights (7.49 of 14.87 GiB) but ~4% of warm latency, and is idle during denoise and decode.
- [findings/vae-decode-sets-peak-memory.md](findings/vae-decode-sets-peak-memory.md) `supported`: For FLUX.2 klein at 1024² on the A100, the VAE decode — not the transformer — sets the peak memory in both PyTorch and sd.cpp.

## Concepts

- [concepts/activation-precision.md](concepts/activation-precision.md): The dtype intermediate tensors are kept in between ops (BF16, FP16, FP32) — separate from weight precision, and a source of conversion overhead and numerical differences across engines.
- [concepts/cross-step-reuse.md](concepts/cross-step-reuse.md): Reusing computation across denoising steps — exact reuse of step-independent values, or approximate caching (TeaCache, DiCache) of slowly changing features.
- [concepts/flash-attention.md](concepts/flash-attention.md): Fused attention kernels that avoid materialising the S×S score matrix; implementations differ a lot in speed for long non-causal sequences.
- [concepts/flow-matching-sampler.md](concepts/flow-matching-sampler.md): FLUX.2's sampling — Euler steps along a flow-matching trajectory with a resolution-dependent timestep shift (mu); defines what "4 steps" means.
- [concepts/im2col-convolution.md](concepts/im2col-convolution.md): Lowering a convolution to a matrix multiply by unrolling input patches into a large matrix (im2col); simple, but memory- and bandwidth-heavy at high resolution.
- [concepts/initial-noise.md](concepts/initial-noise.md): The starting latent of a diffusion sample; the same integer seed gives different noise in different frameworks, so cross-engine image comparisons need shared noise.
- [concepts/stage-residency.md](concepts/stage-residency.md): Which pipeline components' weights and buffers are resident on the device during each stage (text encode, denoise, decode) — a memory-planning choice.
